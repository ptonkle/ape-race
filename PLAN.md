# Plan

Stage 2, written by the agent from `BRIEF.md`. `B2 Require step-by-step justification`.

The brief approves the plan in advance and puts the game in autonomous mode ("Execute in autonomous mode. Do not bug me"). This plan is written, and the build follows it in this order. The brief says the **feel of the physics** comes first, so the swing is built, tested and tuned before anything else is polished.

Technique references, used for how and not for style: Mandible (`~/slop/mandible`, the person's own game) for the online transport, the lobby flow, the volume slider and sound synthesis; sgilson7's Builder games (ribbit-king, iron-vector, MIT) for running WebGL 2 under Builder's rules.

| # | Decision | Reason | Test that shows it holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | **Numbers.** The core uses Q16.16 fixed point in `i32` (`fx.rs`: `ONE = 65536` is one metre, one second, or 1.0). Products go through `i64`. Angles use 65,536 units to a turn, with an integer Taylor-series sine. `geom.rs` holds `V3` (vectors), `Affine` (3×4 matrices) and `Mat4` (4×4 matrices). | BUILDER-SPEC D2 bans floats in the core. Q16.16 resolves 15 µm and reaches ±32 km, more than a 2 km course needs. | Unit tests: `sin` at 0°, 30° and 90°; `mul`/`div` round trips; `norm` gives length `ONE`; a rotation keeps length. |
| P2 | **Rendering.** WebGL 2 instanced drawing of a few unit meshes (cube, sphere, cylinder, cone, blob). Each shape is one instance: a 3×4 matrix plus a colour. Static scenery (trees, ground, branches) is uploaded once per course; dynamic shapes (apes, vines, items, effects) are sent every frame. The core builds the meshes, the instances, the camera matrix and the draw list as Q16 integers. The shim converts them to `f32` by dividing by `ONE`, which is a translation, not a decision. Shaders live in `data/shaders/` and the page only uploads and draws. | R6: the page draws what the core returns. D3: page JavaScript holds no numbers, so every stride, offset and size comes from the core's `numbers()`. A z-buffered GPU draw is the only way to get a full-resolution 3D jungle at 60 fps. | Core tests for mesh vertex counts and normals, and for the camera projecting its look-at point to the screen centre. The browser check confirms a frame is drawn in a WebGL 2 context (the canvas is not blank). |
| P3 | **The ape is a point mass** with a radius, in one of four states: **Swinging** (holding a vine), **Flying**, **Grounded** or **Stunned**. The core steps at a fixed 120 ticks per second; the page passes its clock and the core runs as many ticks as are due, then draws interpolated positions. | Fixed steps give the same physics on every machine and screen rate, and 120 Hz keeps a fast pendulum stable. | Two runs with the same inputs give equal states; frame rate does not change the outcome (stepping 1 frame of 16 ms equals 2 frames of 8 ms). |
| P4 | **The swing.** A grabbed vine is a rope from its anchor. The grab length is the distance from the anchor to the ape's hands when it grabs, clamped to the vine's length. Each tick: gravity, then air drag, then the position is projected back onto the rope's sphere when the rope is taut and the outward radial velocity is removed. On the grab, the ape's speed is redirected along the swing at 96% of its size (**Grab Keep**), so a good grab keeps momentum. **W/S** pump along the swing direction (up to **Pump**), and **A/D** lean the swing plane sideways (**Steer**). Releasing Space lets go with the current velocity, plus a small **Release Boost** along it when it is released on the upswing. | This is what the brief describes: momentum is built by grabbing and letting go at good times, and is kept "in a fun but balanced way". Quadratic drag sets a soft top speed without a hard cap, so faster always feels faster. | Tests: energy is near-conserved over a free swing (no pumping, no drag): the height of the swing's top stays within 3%. Pumping in phase raises the swing's peak. A release at the bottom of the arc keeps its speed. Grab Keep keeps 96% of the speed. Speed under drag tends to a top speed and does not run away. |
| P5 | **Grab timing.** Pressing Space opens a **Grab Window** (0.15 s). If a vine comes within **Reach** during that window while Space is still held, the ape grabs it. If none does, the press is a **Missed Grab**, and Space cannot grab again for the **Grab Cooldown** (0.45 s). Holding Space from before does nothing: only a fresh press grabs. | Decision 2 (the person's answer): a missed grab starts a cooldown, so spamming the key does not work. The short window still forgives a press made a frame early. | Tests: a press with a vine in reach grabs; a press with none starts the cooldown; a second press within the cooldown does not grab even when a vine is in reach; a press after the cooldown does grab; holding Space through a vine does not grab it. |
| P6 | **The ground.** An ape that touches the ground lands and becomes Grounded. It runs with WASD at **Run Speed** (slow next to swinging), and its leftover speed is lost to **Ground Friction**. Space on the ground is a jump that also opens a Grab Window lasting until the top of the jump, and a jump never counts as a Missed Grab. Low vines on the course are in reach from a jump, so a fallen ape must find one and climb back up. | Brief: "If you fall to the ground you need to find a vine to start swinging again". | Tests: landing becomes Grounded; ground speed falls to Run Speed; a jump under a low vine grabs it. |
| P7 | **The course.** One hand-authored jungle course (`course.rs`). It is a centreline of control points that runs from a start tree to a finish arch and never returns to the start, with turns (decision 3). Vines hang from branches at different heights, sparse enough that a straight line of vines all the way is impossible, and are placed by hand-tuned rules from a fixed seed so the course is the same every time. There are at most 5 moving parts: swaying vines and a swinging log. Walls of dense trunks line the corridor, and a soft wall pushes an ape back in. Progress along the centreline gives race position and the minimap. The data model allows a later course to be a lap circuit. | Brief: one predesigned jungle course, with turns, sparse vines, different heights, at most about 5 moving parts, laps left possible. | Tests: the course turns (its heading changes by more than 60° in total); no vine is more than about 40 m from the next one along the course; vine heights vary by at least 6 m; no more than 5 moving parts; the AI finishes in 60–120 s (P12). |
| P8 | **Apes.** Six kinds: Orangutan, Gorilla, Chimpanzee, Gibbon, Proboscis Monkey (with a big floppy nose that sways with motion) and Lemur. Each has five stats: **Pump** (swing power), **Top Speed** (lower drag), **Reach** (grab distance), **Air Control** (steering in flight), **Weight** (resistance to hits, and slower on the ground). The stats are different but balanced: every kind's sum is the same. The **Equal Stats** option gives every kind the average. The selection screen shows a spinning model of the chosen ape, with its stats as bars. | Brief section 8. | Tests: the six kinds' stat sums are equal; with Equal Stats a Gorilla and a Lemur have identical physics (the same inputs give the same path). |
| P9 | **Items.** Item crates on the course give one random item when touched. A player holds at most one, and using it (left mouse button or E) consumes it. **Banana:** thrown forward along the camera's aim; an ape it hits is Stunned briefly and loses 40% of its speed. **Stick:** a melee swing that hits an ape within 3.5 m, knocks it off its vine and takes 30% of its speed. **Hook:** fires at the nearest ape ahead within 60 m and pulls it back toward the user, cutting its speed, and gives the user some of that speed. **Speed Boost:** a burst of momentum in the direction the ape is already moving (decision 1). Items get basic behaviour only, as the brief asks. | Brief section 7 and decision 1. | Tests: holding one item blocks a second pickup; use consumes it; a Banana hit cuts the target's speed; a Speed Boost adds speed along the velocity; the Hook picks the nearest ape ahead, not one behind. |
| P10 | **Camera.** Third person, behind the ape. The mouse turns it (pointer lock, with a sensitivity setting) and it eases back behind the direction of travel when the mouse is still. Field of view widens with speed, and speed lines and wind add to the sense of speed. Fog closes in at about 150 m, about 5 s ahead at a high speed. WASD act relative to the camera. | Brief sections 4, 5 and 9. | Test: the camera's projection puts the ape near the screen centre; the field of view at top speed is wider than at rest. |
| P11 | **Race flow.** Countdown 3-2-1, then the race. Finish order comes from crossing the finish arch, and the race clock runs in hundredths. The results show place, ape and time. Then **Rematch** or **Main Menu**. The HUD shows Place, Time, Speed, the held item and an overhead minimap (decision 4). | Brief sections 3 and 11. | Tests: an ape that crosses the finish is placed in order; place during the race follows progress. |
| P12 | **AI.** An AI ape uses the same physics and the same keys as a player, with no cheats on movement. It aims for the next vine along the course, presses grab when a vine is in reach, and lets go when a predicted flight from that moment passes within reach of the next vine (the core simulates the flight ahead). It uses items at simple times. There is one difficulty, as the brief allows. | Brief: AI support, no time spent balancing difficulties. Reusing the physics keeps the AI fair. | Test: an AI completes the course in 60–120 s, which also checks P7's race length. |
| P13 | **Online, up to 4 players.** Mandible's `online.js` is reused: WebRTC, introduced by room code over the pinned Nostr relays, with the same Builder exemptions as Mandible (recorded in BUILDER-SPEC). Unlike Mandible, there is **no lockstep**. Each browser simulates its own ape and sends its state 20 times a second. Other apes are drawn interpolated about 100 ms behind. The host runs the lobby (seats, apes, Equal Stats, AI count, Ready), the countdown and the AI apes, whose states it sends too. Item hits are sent to the victim's browser, which applies them to its own ape. Finish times are reported to the host, which publishes the results. | A twitchy physics racer cannot wait for every player's input each tick. Owning your own ape keeps your controls lag-free, and a race needs no shared simulation beyond positions and hits. The person allowed deviating from Mandible's netcode where it does not fit. | Core tests over a loopback of 2–4 sessions: the lobby seats players; Start reaches everyone; remote apes move; a Banana thrown by one session stuns the other; finish times reach the host and every results screen agrees. Browser test: two tabs race by pasted codes, with no relay used. |
| P14 | **Sound.** The core synthesises each cue as a WAV file, as in Mandible, and the page only plays it: grab, release, missed grab, land, item pickup, throw, hit, boost, countdown beeps, start, finish, plus a wind loop whose volume follows speed. A volume slider is in Settings. | Brief: basic sound effects for now. | Tests: every cue gives a valid WAV header; at 0% volume every sample is silent. |
| P15 | **Menus and settings.** Main Menu: **Race** (vs AI), **Online**, **Settings**. The setup screen has ape choice (with the spinning model), number of AI opponents (0–3) and Equal Stats. Settings: Volume, Mouse Sensitivity, Invert Mouse Y. These are kept in browser storage. Esc opens a pause menu (Resume, Restart, Quit; offline only pauses the race). | Brief sections 8 and 13 (menus and volume slider "available in all games"). | Browser interactions: the menu opens; a race starts against AI; the ape model spins; settings survive a reload. |
| P16 | **Deploy and thumbnail.** `scripts/pages.sh` builds and copies the game into `~/slop/ptonkle.github.io/ape-race/`. A lineup screen (`?lineup`) renders all six apes side by side; a Playwright script screenshots it into `img/ape-race/lineup.webp`, and the home page gets an Ape Race card. Then the site is committed and pushed. | Brief "Done means": the game is deployed on the person's GitHub page, with a basic screenshot of all the ape models as the thumbnail. | The live-site browser check (`BUILDER_ORIGIN=https://ptonkle.github.io/ape-race`) passes in three engines. |
